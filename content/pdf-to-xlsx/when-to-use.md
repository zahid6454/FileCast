## Common Scenarios for PDF to Excel Conversion

### Rebuilding a Budget from a Bank Statement

Your bank exports statements as PDFs, not spreadsheets. Converting to Excel gets each transaction into its own row so you can categorize spending, sum totals, and build a real budget instead of retyping every line by hand.

### Combining Multiple Invoices into One Total

You've got a folder of vendor invoices, each a separate PDF. Converting each one to Excel gives you real numeric cells you can paste into a single running total, instead of adding them up by hand from a screen full of PDFs.

### Analyzing a Report Someone Only Sent as a PDF

A colleague or client sends a data-heavy report as a PDF because that's what their system exports. Converting to Excel lets you sort, filter, and pivot the numbers instead of just reading them.

### Digitizing Printed Price Lists or Catalogs

Suppliers often distribute price lists as PDFs. Converting to Excel turns the SKU and price columns into real data you can import into inventory or pricing software, rather than manually copying each line.

### Archiving Records in a Working Format

Old reports and statements that only exist as PDFs are hard to search or aggregate. Converting them to Excel makes years of records queryable in one workbook.
