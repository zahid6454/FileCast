## Common Scenarios for Reordering PDF Pages

### Fixing Scans Done Out of Sequence

Feeding paper into a scanner out of order, or scanning both sides of a double-sided document separately, often produces a PDF with pages in the wrong sequence. Reordering puts them back the way the original document was meant to be read.

### Restructuring a Merged Document

Combining files with [PDF Merge](/convert/pdf-merge) stacks pages in the order the source files were added, which isn't always the order the final document needs. Reordering afterward lets you fix the structure — moving an appendix to the end, or a summary to the front — without redoing the merge.

### Moving a Table of Contents or Summary to the Front

A document assembled piece by piece sometimes ends up with its table of contents or executive summary buried partway through instead of at the start. Reordering moves it to where readers expect to find it.

### Preparing a Custom Reading Order for a Presentation Packet

When combining several documents into one packet for a meeting, the most logical reading order for attendees isn't always the order the files were originally in. Reordering lets you sequence the packet the way you actually want it presented. Assigning the finished packet a reference ID with our [UUID Generator](/convert/uuid-generator) can help keep track of different versions as you refine the order.

### Correcting Page Order After a Double-Sided Print/Scan Round-Trip

Printing a document double-sided and then scanning it back in sometimes reverses or interleaves the page order depending on the scanner's settings. Reordering restores the pages to their intended sequence.
