## Resized vs Original — When to Resize

Resizing changes the pixel dimensions of your image, which affects both its display size and file size. A smaller image naturally produces a smaller file, but the key decision is whether you need the full resolution or a more practical size. Here's how they compare.

| Feature | Original Size | Resized |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensions | Full resolution from camera or source | Custom width and height you specify |
| File size | Often unnecessarily large | Smaller, proportional to new dimensions |
| Display quality | More detail than most screens can show | Matches the size it will actually be viewed at |
| Loading speed | Slower in web pages and apps | Faster to load and render |
| Editing flexibility | Maximum data for cropping and adjustments | Less room for heavy editing or large re-enlargements |

### Keep Original Size When

- You plan to print the image at a large size and need full resolution
- You may need to crop a specific area and still have enough detail
- The image is already close to the dimensions you need
- You're archiving photos and want to preserve every pixel for the future
- The file will be used in professional design or video production

### Resize When

- A website, app, or profile requires specific pixel dimensions
- You're sending images by email or chat and the files are too large
- Your web page loads slowly because of oversized images
- You need a thumbnail or preview version of a larger photo
- You're preparing images for a slide presentation or document
- The image is 4000+ pixels wide but will only ever be displayed at a few hundred pixels
