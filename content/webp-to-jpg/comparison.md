## WebP vs JPG — When to Use Each

Both WebP and JPG are popular image formats, but they have different strengths. Here's how they compare:

| Feature | WebP | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| File size | Smaller (25-35% less than JPG) | Larger |
| Image quality | Excellent at small file sizes | Excellent, widely trusted |
| Compatibility | Modern browsers and newer apps | Works on virtually everything |
| Browser support | All major browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge) | All browsers, including older ones |
| Editing support | Limited in many photo editors | Supported by every photo editor |
| Transparency | Supported | Not supported |
| Best for | Websites and online use | Sharing, printing, and everyday use |

### Keep WebP When

- You're using the image on a website or blog
- File size matters and you want the smallest file possible
- You need transparency in a compressed image
- The image will stay on your own computer and your apps can open it
- You're working with a platform that accepts WebP

### Convert to JPG When

- You need to share the image with others and want it to open anywhere
- You're uploading to a site or form that doesn't accept WebP
- You want to edit the image in a photo app that doesn't support WebP
- You need to print the image through an online printing service
- You're attaching the image to an email and want it to display correctly
- You're saving photos for long-term storage in a universal format
