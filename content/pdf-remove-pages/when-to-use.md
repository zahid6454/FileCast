### Deleting Blank or Scanned Junk Pages

Scanned documents often pick up blank pages, cover sheets from the scanner itself, or duplicate scans of the same page. Removing them cleans up the file without having to rebuild it from scratch.

</div>

### Dropping Outdated Cover Pages or Sections

A document that's been revised over time sometimes carries an old cover page, a superseded introduction, or a section that no longer applies. Removing those pages keeps the file current without touching everything else.

</div>
</div>

### Preparing an External-Safe Version of an Internal Document

Internal reports sometimes include pages meant only for internal review — notes, drafts of a section, internal commentary — that shouldn't go out with the external version. Removing those pages before sending keeps the rest of the document intact. Once the redacted copy is finalized, our [Hash Generator](/convert/hash-generator) can produce a checksum so you can confirm later that the external copy you shared hasn't been altered.

</div>

### Trimming a Long Document Down to What's Needed

Not every recipient needs every section of a lengthy document. Removing the sections that don't apply to a particular audience keeps what you send relevant, without going through the extra step of picking out and reassembling only the pages you want to keep.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-edit"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Cleaning Up a Merged PDF

When combining files with [PDF Merge](/convert/pdf-merge), the result sometimes includes pages you didn't actually want in the final combined document — a title page from one of the source files, for example. Removing those afterward finishes the cleanup.
