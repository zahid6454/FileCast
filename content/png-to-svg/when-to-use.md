## Common Scenarios for PNG to SVG Conversion

### Scaling a Low-Resolution Logo for Print or Merchandise

You only have an old, small PNG of a logo, but you need it printed large on a banner, sign, or T-shirt. Vectorizing it into an SVG means it can scale up to any size without turning blurry or blocky.

### Making an Icon Sharp on High-DPI Displays

A PNG icon designed for old, lower-resolution screens looks soft on today's retina and high-DPI displays. Converting it to SVG gives you one file that renders crisply at every screen density, instead of exporting a separate PNG for each size.

### Editing a Logo's Shapes or Colors

You need to tweak a logo — change one color, adjust a shape, or simplify a detail — but only have a flattened PNG, not the original design file. Vectorizing it gives you real, editable shapes you can open and modify in Illustrator, Inkscape, or Figma.

### Building Crisp Graphics for a Website

Icons and logos embedded as SVG stay sharp at any zoom level and any screen size, with a smaller file size than a PNG large enough to look good when scaled up. Vectorizing your existing PNG assets is faster than redesigning them from scratch.

### Adding a Vector Logo to a Printed Document

Once your logo is a scalable SVG, you'll often want it in a printable letterhead, cover page, or business document. Our [Image to PDF](/convert/image-to-pdf/) tool can combine your logo with other pages into a single printable PDF.
