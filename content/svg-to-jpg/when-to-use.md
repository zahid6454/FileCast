## Common Scenarios for SVG to JPG Conversion

### Uploading Logos to Social Media or Profiles

Most social media platforms — Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X — don't accept SVG files for profile pictures, cover photos, or post images. Converting your logo or graphic to JPG lets you upload it without issues.

### Printing

Print shops and online print services typically want images in JPG or PDF format. Converting your SVG to a high-resolution JPG ensures your printed materials — business cards, flyers, posters — come out looking crisp and professional. Once you have the JPG, our [JPG to PDF converter](/convert/jpg-to-pdf/) can bundle several of them into a single print-ready document.

### Sharing with Others

Not everyone has software that can open SVG files. If you're sending an image to a client, coworker, or friend, JPG is the safest bet. It opens in any image viewer on any device — phones, tablets, and computers alike.

### Using in Documents and Emails

Word processors, email editors, and older document tools work best with standard image formats. If you're inserting a logo into a letter or placing a graphic in an email newsletter, JPG is the format that won't give you trouble.

### Reducing File Size for Complex Graphics

A highly detailed SVG with many shapes, gradients, or filters can sometimes be heavier than a compressed JPG of the same image. Converting flattens all that vector data into a single, predictably sized photo.
