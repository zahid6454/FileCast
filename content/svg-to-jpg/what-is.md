## What Is SVG?

SVG stands for Scalable Vector Graphics. Unlike regular images made of tiny dots (pixels), SVG files are built from mathematical shapes — lines, curves, and colors described by code. This means an SVG image can be scaled up to the size of a billboard or shrunk down to a tiny icon, and it will always look perfectly sharp.

SVGs are the go-to format for logos, icons, illustrations, and diagrams. If you've ever seen a company logo that looks crisp on both a phone screen and a large banner, there's a good chance it started as an SVG.

### Why Convert to JPG?

JPG is a fixed-size, universally supported photo format. Every device, app, browser, and printing service can open a JPG without hesitation — SVG support is far less consistent, especially in older software, email clients, and document editors. Converting flattens the vector shapes into pixels at a specific size, trading infinite scalability for guaranteed compatibility.
