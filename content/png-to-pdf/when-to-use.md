### Documenting a Bug or Issue

Screenshots are the standard way to show what went wrong in an app or website. Combining a sequence of PNG screenshots into a single PDF creates a clear, ordered report you can attach to a support ticket or send to a developer. If the report is heavier than you'd like, run your screenshots through [Image Compressor](/convert/image-compress/) before combining them.

### Submitting Scanned Documents

Many government agencies, banks, and employers require documents in PDF format. If you've scanned your ID, receipts, or forms as PNG images, converting them to a single PDF makes them ready to upload without any hassle.

### Sharing Design Mockups

Designers often export mockups, wireframes, or diagrams as PNGs. Combining a set of related screens or slides into one PDF creates a document a client or teammate can page through in order, rather than opening a dozen separate image files.

</div>
</div>

### Building Step-by-Step Guides

Tutorials and how-to guides often rely on a sequence of annotated screenshots. Converting them into a single PDF keeps every step in the correct order and turns loose images into a document that's easy to print or file away.

</div>
</div>

### Sending Screenshots by Email

Email attachments work best with fewer files. Instead of attaching ten separate PNGs to an email, converting them to a single PDF gives your recipient one clean download and avoids the risk of screenshots arriving out of order.
