## Common Scenarios for Splitting PDF Files

### Extracting a Single Form or Page

Government and corporate documents often bundle several forms into one PDF. If you only need to fill out and submit one of them, splitting saves you from printing or forwarding the entire package. Pull out the specific form, and you have a clean file ready to use.

### Sharing Sections of a Report

A 60-page annual report might contain financial data, project summaries, and team updates. Your finance team only needs the budget section, while your project managers need the timeline pages. Splitting lets you send each group exactly the pages relevant to them — nothing more, nothing less.

### Reducing File Size for Email

Email attachment limits are a constant headache. When a PDF is too large to send, splitting it into smaller parts gets around the size restriction. Each piece stays under the limit, and the recipient can easily reassemble them if needed.

### Removing Sensitive Pages Before Sharing

Legal documents, medical records, and internal reports often contain pages that shouldn't leave your desk. Instead of redacting content within a page, you can simply exclude those pages entirely and share a version that only contains what's appropriate for the audience.

### Creating Study Materials

Students and professionals regularly pull individual chapters or sections from textbooks, training manuals, and reference guides. Splitting a large PDF into topic-specific files makes it easier to organize study sessions, annotate specific sections, and share relevant material with classmates or colleagues.
