## JPG vs PNG — When to Use Each

Both JPG and PNG are popular image formats, but they serve different purposes. Here is a quick breakdown of how they compare.

| Feature | JPG | PNG |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Photos and real-world images | Graphics, logos, and screenshots |
| File size | Smaller | Larger |
| Compression | Lossy (removes some data) | Lossless (keeps all data) |
| Transparency | Not supported | Fully supported |
| Image quality | Good for photos, softens edges | Crisp and pixel-perfect |
| Color depth | Up to 16.7 million colors | Up to 16.7 million colors + alpha |
| Editing | Quality drops with repeated saves | Quality stays the same every time |

### Keep JPG When

- You are sharing personal photos by email or messaging apps
- You need to keep file sizes as small as possible
- The image is a photograph with no need for transparency
- You are uploading to social media, which often converts to JPG anyway
- Storage space is a concern and slight quality loss is acceptable

### Convert to PNG When

- You need a transparent background on your image
- You are working with logos, icons, or graphics with sharp edges
- You plan to edit the image multiple times and want to preserve quality
- The image contains text or fine lines that need to stay crisp
- You are preparing assets for a website, app, or design project
- You want to use the image as an overlay or layer in another design
