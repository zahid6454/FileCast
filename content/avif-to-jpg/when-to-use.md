## Common Scenarios for AVIF to JPG Conversion

### Uploading to Older Software or Platforms

Many photo editors, e-commerce listing tools, and content management systems still don't accept AVIF uploads. Converting to JPG first avoids a confusing "unsupported file type" error.

### Printing Photos

Online and in-store photo printing services almost universally expect JPG. If you've downloaded or been sent AVIF images, converting them first ensures your print order actually goes through.

### Sharing with Older Devices

Not everyone's phone, tablet, or desktop software can open an AVIF file, especially on older operating systems. JPG guarantees the recipient can view the image without hunting for the right app.

### Archiving Converted Photos

If you're batch-converting a set of AVIF photos and want to keep them as a single, easy-to-browse file, combine the resulting JPGs into one document with [Image to PDF](/convert/image-to-pdf/) afterward.

### Editing in Software Without AVIF Support

Some image editors — particularly older versions or budget tools — either can't open AVIF at all or only support a limited subset of it. Converting to JPG first sidesteps compatibility issues entirely.

### Email Attachments

Some email clients and corporate mail filters flag or strip unfamiliar attachment types. JPG is universally recognized and rarely causes delivery issues.
