## Common Scenarios for Adding Page Numbers to a PDF

### Preparing Reports for Printing

A printed report without page numbers is easy to reorder by accident, especially once it's out of its original binder or folder. Adding numbers before printing keeps the pages easy to put back in order if they're ever shuffled.

### Numbering Merged Documents

Combining several PDFs into one packet — a proposal with appendices, a case file with multiple exhibits — often means each original file had its own numbering, or none. Adding a single consistent numbering pass afterward makes the combined document readable as one whole. Our [PDF Organize](/convert/pdf-organize) tool is useful for getting the page order right before numbering, and printed handouts of the finished packet are often easier to track at events with a scannable code from our [Barcode Generator](/convert/barcode-generator).

### Creating Handouts for Meetings or Events

Printed handouts that reference "turn to page 3" only work if page 3 is labeled as such. Numbering the pages before printing avoids confusion during the meeting itself.

</div>
</div>

<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-page"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Formatting Manuscripts or Long-Form Documents

Manuscripts, theses, and other long documents typically need page numbers for citation and navigation, sometimes with a specific starting number (skipping a title page, for example). Custom start numbers make that straightforward.

</div>
</div>

### Finalizing Legal or Contractual Documents

Contracts and legal filings often need every page numbered for reference in signatures, exhibits, or amendments. Consistent numbering also makes it easy to confirm no pages are missing before the document is signed or filed.
