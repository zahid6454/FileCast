## Common Scenarios for Flattening a PDF

### Finalizing a Completed Form

Once someone has filled out a form — an application, a consent form, an intake sheet — flattening locks in their answers before the file is filed or forwarded, preventing any accidental or intentional edits afterward. If you want a timestamped reference for exactly when it was finalized, our [Unix Timestamp Converter](/convert/unix-timestamp-converter) can generate one to include in your own records alongside the file.

### Archiving Signed Documents

Digitally signed contracts and agreements are often built on fillable PDF forms. Flattening after signing turns the signature and filled fields into permanent content, appropriate for long-term archival where editability is no longer wanted.

### Distributing a Filled Template Without Making It Editable

If you've filled out a template with information specific to one recipient — a personalized certificate, a customized proposal — flattening before sending keeps the recipient from altering what you filled in, while everything still displays and prints correctly.

### Ensuring Consistent Display Across Viewers

Interactive form fields don't always render identically in every PDF viewer, especially on mobile or in browser-based viewers with limited form support. Flattening removes that inconsistency by baking the visible result into the page itself.

### Preparing a Form for Print-Only Use

If a form's next step is being printed and physically filed rather than edited digitally, there's no more need for the fields to stay interactive. Flattening simplifies the file down to exactly what will end up on paper.
