## Common Scenarios for HEIC to JPG Conversion

<div class="scenario-list" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-user"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Sharing on Social Media

Most social platforms accept JPG but not HEIC. If you're posting iPhone photos to Instagram, Twitter, Facebook, or LinkedIn from a desktop, converting to JPG first avoids upload errors.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-mail"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Email Attachments

When you email a HEIC photo to someone on Windows or Android, they often can't open it. Converting to JPG ensures the recipient can view the image regardless of their device.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-upload-cloud"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Uploading to Websites

Online forms, job applications, e-commerce listings, and CMS platforms typically expect JPG or PNG files. HEIC uploads are usually rejected silently or with a confusing error.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-refresh"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Windows and Android Users

Transferring iPhone photos to a Windows PC or Android device via USB or cloud storage often results in a folder full of .heic files that won't open without installing additional software. Batch-converting to JPG before the transfer solves this.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-printer"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Photo Printing Services

Online print services — canvas prints, photo books, greeting cards — almost universally require JPG. HEIC files are rejected at upload.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-edit"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Professional Workflows

If you're handing off photos to a client, colleague, or designer, JPG is the safe default. Not everyone has the software to open HEIC files, and asking recipients to install extra software creates friction.

</div>
