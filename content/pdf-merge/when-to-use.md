## Common Scenarios for Merging PDF Files

### Submitting Job Applications

Many job portals ask for a single PDF containing your resume, cover letter, and any supporting documents. Instead of hoping the hiring manager opens three separate attachments in the right order, merging them guarantees your application reads as one cohesive package from start to finish.

### Assembling Reports and Proposals

Business proposals often pull together sections from different team members — an executive summary, a technical breakdown, a budget spreadsheet exported to PDF, and a timeline. Merging these into a single document creates a polished, professional deliverable that's easy to distribute and review.

### Combining Scanned Documents

Scanners often produce one PDF per page or per batch. If you've scanned a multi-page contract, medical form, or set of receipts across several sessions, merging brings all the pages together into the document they were meant to be.

### Preparing Academic Submissions

Students and researchers regularly need to submit assignments, research papers, or grant applications as a single file. When your paper, bibliography, and appendices live in different PDFs, merging ensures the submission meets the upload requirements without any last-minute formatting headaches.

### Organizing Personal Records

Tax season, insurance claims, and mortgage applications all involve gathering documents from multiple sources — bank statements, receipts, identification, and signed forms. Merging these into one PDF per category keeps your personal records tidy and makes them easy to share with accountants, agents, or anyone else who needs the full picture.
