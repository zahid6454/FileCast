## Common Scenarios for BMP to JPG Conversion

### Sharing Old Scanned Images

If you've scanned old family photos, documents, or printed pictures, your scanner may have saved them as BMP files. These scans are often far too large to send to someone by email or through a messaging app. Converting them to JPG shrinks the files down so you can easily share them with family and friends.

### Reducing Massive File Sizes

BMP files are uncompressed, which means a single image can easily be 10, 20, or even 50 megabytes. That adds up fast if you have a folder full of them. Converting to JPG can reduce each file by 90% or more, turning a 20 MB image into one that's just 1 or 2 MB while still looking great.

### Uploading to Websites

Most websites, online stores, and social media platforms expect images in JPG format. Many won't even accept BMP files at all. If you need to add a photo to your profile, list a product for sale, or post something online, converting to JPG first makes the upload quick and painless.

### Sending Email Attachments

Email services usually limit attachment sizes to somewhere between 10 and 25 megabytes. A handful of BMP files can easily blow past that limit. JPG versions of those same images will be small enough to attach without any trouble, and they'll download faster on the other end too.

### Freeing Up Storage Space

If your computer, phone, or external drive is filling up and you have a collection of BMP images, converting them to JPG is one of the easiest ways to reclaim space. You'll get the same images in a fraction of the size, and you won't notice any difference when viewing them on screen.
