### Speeding Up Your Website

Large PNG files slow down web pages. Converting your site's images to WebP can cut load times noticeably, which improves the experience for visitors and can help with search engine rankings.

</div>

### Reducing Storage Usage

If you have a large collection of PNG screenshots or graphics, converting them to WebP frees up significant storage space. The images still look the same on screen, but each file takes up considerably less room.

</div>
</div>

### Preparing Images for Web Projects

When building a website, blog, or online portfolio, WebP is the preferred format for balancing quality and performance. Converting your PNGs to WebP before uploading keeps your project lean and fast.

</div>
</div>

### Sharing Graphics With Transparency

PNG is often chosen for its transparency support, but WebP supports transparency too — at a smaller file size. If you're sharing transparent graphics online, WebP gives you the same result in a lighter package.

</div>
</div>

<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-mail"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Optimizing Email and Messaging

Smaller image files load faster in email clients and messaging apps. Converting a PNG to WebP before attaching it means quicker delivery and less bandwidth used, while the image still looks sharp.
