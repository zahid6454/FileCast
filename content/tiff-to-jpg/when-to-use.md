## Common Scenarios for TIFF to JPG Conversion

### Sharing Scanned Documents

Office scanners and multifunction printers often save scans as TIFF files. That's great for quality, but it makes sharing a headache. Convert to JPG and your scanned receipts, signed forms, or ID copies become easy to attach to an email or drop into a chat.

</div>

### Emailing Professional Photos

Received a batch of wedding photos, headshots, or product images from a photographer? They're probably in TIFF format. Converting to JPG shrinks the file size dramatically so you can email them without hitting attachment limits or making recipients wait for a massive download.

</div>
</div>

### Uploading to Websites

Most websites and online platforms don't accept TIFF files. Whether you're listing a product on an online store, updating your company website, or adding images to a blog post, you'll need JPG. It loads faster for your visitors too.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-user"></use></svg></div>
<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Viewing on Phones and Tablets

TIFF files often won't open on mobile devices, or they take forever to load. Convert to JPG and you can view, zoom, and scroll through your images smoothly on any phone or tablet — no special app required.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">
<div class="scenario__icon"><svg aria-hidden="true" focusable="false"><use href="#icon-printer"></use></svg></div>

### Printing Through Online Services

Ordering prints, photo books, or custom merchandise from an online service? Most of these platforms accept JPG but not TIFF. A quick conversion gets your images ready to upload without any fuss. The print quality from a high-quality JPG is more than good enough for cards, posters, and photo gifts.
