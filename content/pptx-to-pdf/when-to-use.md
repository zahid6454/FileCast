### Sharing Slides After a Presentation

After delivering a talk, it's common to share your slides with attendees. PDF is the best format for this — everyone can open it regardless of their software, and it strips out animations that don't make sense outside of a live presentation.

### Creating Handouts for Training

Training materials often originate as PowerPoint decks but need to be distributed as printed or digital handouts. PDF produces clean, fixed-layout pages that print predictably and can be annotated by trainees.

</div>

### Sending Sales Decks to Prospects

When you email a sales deck to a potential client, you want it to look polished on their screen. PowerPoint files can render differently depending on the recipient's software and fonts. PDF eliminates that risk entirely.

</div>
</div>

### Submitting Academic Presentations

Conferences and academic institutions often require presentation files in PDF format. Converting your PPTX ensures the submission meets format requirements and preserves your slide layout exactly as designed.

</div>
</div>

### Archiving Completed Presentations

Once a presentation has been delivered and won't be edited again, converting to PDF creates a compact, permanent record. PDF files are ideal for archiving because they're self-contained, universally readable, and don't depend on PowerPoint to open.
