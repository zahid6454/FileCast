## Compressed vs Original — When to Compress

Compression shrinks your image's file size while keeping its dimensions the same. The question isn't whether compression works — it's whether the space savings are worth a small reduction in quality. Here's how compressed and original files compare.

| Feature | Original | Compressed |
|---|---|---|
| File size | Full size (often 2-10 MB for photos) | Significantly smaller (often 50-80% reduction) |
| Visual quality | Maximum detail preserved | Slight quality loss at lower settings, usually unnoticeable above 70% |
| Loading speed | Slower on web pages and email | Faster to load, send, and download |
| Storage impact | Takes more space on disk and cloud | Frees up meaningful storage over many files |
| Editing flexibility | Best for further editing | Fine for viewing and sharing, less ideal for heavy re-editing |

### Keep Original When

- You plan to edit the image again later in design or photo editing software
- The image is already small enough for your needs
- You're archiving important photographs and want maximum detail preserved
- The image contains fine text or intricate line work that needs to stay crisp
- You're preparing files for professional printing where every detail matters

### Compress When

- You're uploading images to a website and want pages to load faster
- Email attachments are too large to send
- You need to stay within a file size limit on an upload form
- You're sharing photos through messaging apps or social media
- Your device or cloud storage is running low on space
- You're building a portfolio or gallery with dozens of images that need to load quickly
