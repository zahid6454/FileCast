## Common Scenarios for Password-Protecting a PDF

### Sending Financial Documents

Tax returns, bank statements, and invoices often travel by email, which isn't a secure channel by default. Adding a password before sending means that even if the email is forwarded to the wrong person or intercepted, the document itself stays locked.

### Sharing HR and Legal Paperwork

Offer letters, contracts, and performance reviews contain details that should stay between the sender and the intended recipient. A password requirement adds a real barrier beyond just "please don't share this."

### Distributing Confidential Reports

Internal reports, board materials, and strategy documents sometimes need to reach a specific group of people without spreading further. Password protection means the file only opens for those who were given the password directly. If you also want to verify a shared copy hasn't been altered afterward, our [Hash Generator](/convert/hash-generator) produces a checksum you can compare against the original.

### Archiving Sensitive Records Long-Term

Files kept in shared drives or backups for years can outlive the access controls that originally protected them. A password baked into the file itself keeps working regardless of how storage permissions change over time.

### Protecting Client Deliverables Before Final Payment

Freelancers and agencies sometimes want to share a preview of finished work without it being fully usable until payment clears. A password held back until payment is confirmed keeps the deliverable inaccessible until you're ready to release it.
