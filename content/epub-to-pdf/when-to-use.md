### Printing a Chapter or an Entire Book

EPUB readers aren't built for printing — text reflows to the screen, not a printed page. Converting to PDF gives you fixed pages that print cleanly, whether you need one chapter for a book club or the whole book for offline reading.

</div>

### Citing a Specific Page

Academic and legal writing often needs a page number to cite. EPUB has no fixed pages to reference, but a converted PDF does, making it possible to cite a specific passage the way you would from a printed book.

</div>

### Reading on a Device Without EPUB Support

Not every device or PDF-only workflow supports EPUB. Converting gives you a format that opens everywhere — no dedicated ebook reader app required.

</div>
</div>

### Annotating with a PDF-Only Tool

Some annotation and markup tools — including many used in classrooms and law offices — only accept PDF. Converting your EPUB first means you can highlight, comment, and mark up the text in tools that never supported EPUB at all.

### Archiving Ebooks in a Universal Format

EPUB readers and DRM schemes change over the years; PDF has stayed readable for decades. Converting your ebook library to PDF is a safer long-term archive format. Once converted, our [Hash Generator](/convert/hash-generator/) can produce a checksum so you can confirm later that your archived copy hasn't changed.
