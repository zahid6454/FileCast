## What Is PDF Watermark?

A watermark is text stamped across every page of a PDF — often diagonally, in a light, semi-transparent style — that stays visible no matter how the page is viewed or printed. Common examples are "CONFIDENTIAL," "DRAFT," or "SAMPLE," but a watermark can be any text you choose, including a name or company.

Unlike a password, a watermark doesn't restrict who can open the file. It changes what the file communicates to anyone who does: this copy is a draft, this document is confidential, or this sample isn't the final deliverable.

### Why Add a Watermark?

Watermarks set expectations at a glance. A reviewer looking at a "DRAFT" watermark knows not to treat the content as final. A client looking at a "SAMPLE" watermark on a work preview understands it's not the finished, licensed version. A "CONFIDENTIAL" watermark reminds anyone who receives the file, even through several forwards, that it wasn't meant for wide distribution.

They're also a lightweight deterrent — a redistributed copy still carries the watermark, making its origin and intended handling obvious.
