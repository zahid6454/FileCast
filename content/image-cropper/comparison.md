## Cropping vs Resizing — When to Use Which

Cropping and resizing both change an image's dimensions, but they do it in very different ways — one trims, the other scales.

| Feature | Cropping | Resizing |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Removes part of the image | Scales the whole image up or down |
| Content | Only the selected area remains | All original content stays, just smaller or larger |
| Quality | No stretching or scaling artifacts | Can introduce softness at extreme scale changes |
| Best for | Removing unwanted background or fitting a specific area | Changing overall file dimensions while keeping everything visible |

### Crop When

- Your photo includes background or people you want to remove
- You need to focus on a specific detail within a larger image
- You're fitting an image into a fixed aspect ratio, like a square profile photo
- Part of the image is irrelevant or distracting

### Resize When

- You want to keep the entire image but change its overall dimensions
- You're meeting a specific width or height requirement without losing any content
- You need a smaller file size without trimming what's shown — FileCast's [Image Resizer](/convert/image-resize/) is built for exactly this
