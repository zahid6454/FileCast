## What Is PDF Compression?

PDF compression reduces the file size of a PDF document by downsampling images, removing redundant data, and optimizing the internal structure. The goal is to make the file smaller while keeping the content readable and visually acceptable. A well-compressed PDF can be a fraction of the original size without obvious quality loss.

### Why Compress PDFs?

Large PDFs are a problem. They're slow to upload, hard to email (most services cap attachments at 10-25MB), and take up unnecessary storage space. Scanned documents, image-heavy reports, and exported presentations often produce PDFs that are far larger than they need to be. Compression brings them down to a practical size.

### When Does Compression Help Most?

The biggest gains come from PDFs that contain high-resolution images — scanned pages, photo-heavy brochures, and exported presentations with embedded graphics. Text-only PDFs are already small and won't shrink much further. If your PDF is mostly text, compression may reduce the file size by only a few percent.
