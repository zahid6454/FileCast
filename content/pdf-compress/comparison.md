## Compression Levels — When to Use Each

| Level | Image DPI | Best For | Typical Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximum (Screen) | 72 dpi | Email, web viewing, quick sharing | 60-90% smaller |
| Balanced (Ebook) | 150 dpi | General sharing, digital reading | 40-70% smaller |
| Moderate (Printer) | 300 dpi | Office printing, reports | 20-40% smaller |
| Minimal (Prepress) | 300 dpi | Professional printing, high quality | 10-20% smaller |

### Use Maximum Compression When

You need the smallest possible file and image quality isn't critical. Scanned documents being emailed, forms for online submission, and reference copies that just need to be readable. Text remains sharp at any compression level — only images are affected.

### Use Balanced or Moderate Compression When

You want a good balance between file size and visual quality. Balanced works well for documents shared digitally — reports, presentations, and proposals. Moderate is the right choice when the document might be printed on a standard office printer.
