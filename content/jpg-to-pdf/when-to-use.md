### Submitting Scanned Documents

Many government agencies, banks, and employers require documents in PDF format. If you've photographed your ID, receipts, or forms as JPGs with your phone, converting them to a single PDF makes them ready to upload without any hassle.

### Archiving Phone Photos

Receipt photos, whiteboard notes, and signed forms pile up fast in a camera roll. Converting a batch of related JPGs into a single PDF per month or per category creates a tidy filing system that's easy to search through later. If the resulting PDF is larger than you'd like to keep around, run it through [Image Compressor](/convert/image-compress/) on your original photos first, before combining them.

### Preparing Presentation Handouts

When you need to share photos of slides, whiteboards, or printed handouts from a meeting, combining them into a PDF keeps everything in the right order. Recipients get one file they can print or review later, rather than a folder of loose photos.

</div>
</div>

### Creating a Photo Portfolio

Photographers and designers often need to share a shoot in a clean, professional format. Combining selected JPGs into a PDF creates a polished document that recipients can scroll through as a single file — no need to download and sort individual photos.

</div>
</div>

### Sending Photos by Email

Email attachments work best with fewer files. Instead of attaching ten separate JPGs to an email, converting them to a single PDF gives your recipient one clean download and avoids the risk of photos arriving out of order.
