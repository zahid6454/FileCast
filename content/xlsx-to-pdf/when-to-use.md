### Sharing Financial Reports

Quarterly earnings, budget summaries, and expense reports often start as spreadsheets but need to be distributed as read-only documents. PDF preserves the table layout, currency formatting, and chart styling while preventing accidental changes to the numbers.

### Printing Data Tables

Spreadsheets are notoriously difficult to print — columns get cut off, page breaks land in awkward places, and headers disappear. Converting to PDF gives you a predictable, print-ready document where every column fits and page breaks are exactly where you expect them.

</div>
</div>

### Submitting Regulatory Documents

Tax filings, compliance reports, and audit documentation often require PDF format. Converting your Excel workbook to PDF creates the clean, official-looking document that regulators and auditors expect to receive.

</div>
</div>

### Emailing Client Deliverables

When you send a spreadsheet to a client, they might see broken formulas, shifted columns, or missing fonts depending on their software. PDF eliminates all of these variables and presents a polished, professional document every time.

</div>
</div>
<div class="scenario" markdown="1">

<div class="scenario__body" markdown="1">

### Archiving Monthly Data

Monthly sales data, inventory counts, and performance metrics that no longer need editing are good candidates for PDF archiving. The converted file is a permanent record that doesn't depend on Excel to open and can't be accidentally modified.
