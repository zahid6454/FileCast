## JPG vs WebP — When to Use Each

Both formats handle photos well, but they shine in different situations.

| Feature | JPG | WebP |
|---|---|---|
| File size | Larger | 25–35% smaller on average |
| Photo quality | Excellent | Excellent (comparable to JPG) |
| Browser support | Universal | All modern browsers |
| Editing tools | Supported everywhere | Supported in most modern editors |
| Older device support | Works on everything | May not open on very old software |
| Transparency | Not supported | Supported |

### Keep JPG When

- You need the widest possible compatibility with older devices and software.
- You are sharing photos with people who may use outdated image viewers.
- You are submitting images to a service or platform that specifically requires JPG.
- You plan to edit the image repeatedly in photo editing software that handles JPG better.
- You are printing photos through a service that only accepts JPG uploads.

### Convert to WebP When

- You are adding images to a website and want pages to load faster.
- You need to reduce bandwidth costs for a site with lots of visitors.
- You want to save storage space without losing visible quality.
- You are building a web app or online project that benefits from smaller assets.
- You are optimizing images for platforms that recommend or prefer WebP.
- You need transparency in a photo-quality image (something JPG cannot do at all).
