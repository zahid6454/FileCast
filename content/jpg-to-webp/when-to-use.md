## Common Scenarios for JPG to WebP Conversion

### Speeding Up Website Load Times

Images are often the heaviest part of a web page. Switching from JPG to WebP can cut image file sizes by 25–35%, which means pages load noticeably faster. Visitors are less likely to leave a slow site, and search engines tend to rank faster pages higher.

### Reducing Bandwidth Usage

Every time someone views an image on your site, it uses bandwidth. If you run a blog, online store, or portfolio with dozens of photos per page, those bytes add up quickly. WebP helps you serve the same visual experience while transferring less data — which can lower hosting costs and improve the experience for visitors on slower connections.

### Optimizing Images for Web Projects

Whether you are building a personal site, a company landing page, or a web application, using WebP keeps your project lean. Smaller images mean quicker builds, faster deploys, and a snappier experience for everyone who uses what you have built.

### Saving Storage Space

If you maintain a large library of images — product photos, blog illustrations, or a gallery — converting to WebP frees up meaningful storage space. The savings grow with the size of your collection. A folder of 1,000 photos could shrink by hundreds of megabytes with no visible quality loss.

### Preparing Images for Modern Web Platforms

Many content management systems, website builders, and hosting platforms now recommend or automatically serve WebP images. Converting your JPGs ahead of time gives you more control over quality settings and ensures your images are ready to go without relying on automatic conversion that you cannot fine-tune.
