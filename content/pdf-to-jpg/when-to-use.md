### Sharing Document Pages on Social Media

Social media platforms accept images but not PDFs. Converting a PDF page to JPG lets you share infographics, charts, certificates, or key pages from a report directly on Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, or Facebook.

### Embedding in Presentations

Slide decks often need a snapshot of a PDF page — a chart from a report, a page from a contract, or a diagram from a manual. Converting to JPG gives you an image you can drag directly into PowerPoint, Google Slides, or Keynote.

</div>

### Sending via Messaging Apps

Many messaging apps handle images better than PDFs — they show inline previews, load faster, and don't require the recipient to download and open a separate viewer. Converting to JPG makes your document instantly viewable in the chat.

</div>
</div>

### Creating Website Thumbnails

If you need to show a preview of a PDF on a website — a report cover, a menu, or a product sheet — converting the first page to JPG gives you a lightweight image that loads fast and works in any browser. For an even smaller thumbnail, run the result through [Image Compressor](/convert/image-compress/).

</div>

### Reducing File Size for Email

JPG images are typically much smaller than the PDF pages they came from. When you only need to share a few pages and file size is a concern, converting to JPG can shrink the attachment size significantly.
